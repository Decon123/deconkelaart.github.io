Semester 08 · Advanced Manufacturing Engineering

Advanced Manufacturing Solutions — Vega Innovations

Team of 3 · Process analysis & solution design

An industrial field study at Vega Innovations that identified three recurring manufacturing problems and matched each to an advanced manufacturing process: hydroforming (Flexform) to replace inaccurate press-brake bending of EV-charger chassis with single-step, low-springback forming; waterjet cutting to trim thermoformed ABS enclosures cleanly with no heat-affected zone — eliminating the edge-melting and burring of CNC routing and holding ±0.1–0.2 mm tolerance; and laser wobble seam welding to produce airtight, low-distortion seams on thin stainless humidifier bodies where MIG/TIG warping and leaks caused repeated rework. Each solution was assessed on working principle, key process parameters, and trade-offs against the conventional and alternative methods.

Semester 08 · Final-Year Research Project

Vibration Energy Harvester for a Stealth GPS Tracker

Team of 3 · Mechanical design, FEA & signal processing

Design and development of an electromagnetic vibration energy harvester to recharge the battery of a vehicle-mounted stealth GPS tracking unit. We instrumented a Toyota Raize lower control arm with an ESP32 + ADXL345 accelerometer logging at 100 Hz, then used MATLAB (Welch PSD) to find the dominant suspension vibration band (~11 Hz). An analytical spring-magnet-coil model was tuned to that frequency and verified in COMSOL Multiphysics — flux-linkage sweeps, a casing/core study, and a time-dependent simulation predicting an ~11.7 V peak coil output, with the analytical model showing a ~1.2 mW power peak at the 11 Hz resonance. A core-less prototype was then fabricated and tested.

Semester 08 · Industrial Engineering

Gel Pen Assembly Line Improvement — Atlas Axillia

Team of 5 · Time study, line balancing & plant simulation

An industrial-engineering study of the gel pen assembly line at Atlas Axillia's Peliyagoda plant. Shop-floor time studies pinpointed manual sticker pasting (1,212 units/hr) and card packing as the bottlenecks capping the line at ~1,700 units/hr with 55.96% line efficiency. We built a Tecnomatix Plant Simulation model that reproduced the throughput, then proposed three automation solutions — a rotary sticker applicator and a semi-automated pen-to-card loading fixture among them — with cost-benefit analyses projecting payback in roughly 6–8 months.

Semester 07 · MEMS & Nanotechnology

MEMS Pressure Sensor Array

Individual · MEMS design & FEA simulation

Designed a MEMS piezoresistive pressure-sensor array for real-time gait and posture monitoring in wearable footwear. Each cell uses a silicon diaphragm with p-type piezoresistors in a Wheatstone bridge and an isolation trench for independent movement, arrayed to map plantar pressure across the sole. Simulated in COMSOL (solid mechanics + electric currents), confirming a linear pressure-to-voltage response, mechanically safe von Mises stress and modal stability.

External Project · IoT & Full-Stack Web

WaterIT — Smart Plant Watering & Monitoring

Individual project · Arduino firmware, Flask backend & dashboard UI

An automated plant-watering system that closes the loop between hardware and a live web dashboard. An Arduino UNO reads a soil-moisture sensor and drives a relay-controlled pump (with a servo indicator) to water only when the soil runs dry. A Flask backend ingests the sensor stream over USB serial, logs soil moisture and per-cycle water use to SQLite, and pulls live temperature and humidity from the OpenWeatherMap API. The front end serves a real-time dashboard (2 second polling) plus a Chart.js analytics page tracking moisture, watering events and climate over time, while a Google Gemini integration turns the logged data into plant-care insights. Water consumption is derived from pump run-time at a 0.0025 L/s flow rate.

External Project · Mechanical Design & FEA

Semi-Automatic Tile Installing Machine

Individual project · Concept design, CAD modelling & FEA validation

A trolley-based, semi-automatic machine that mechanises floor-tile laying for small contractors and DIY users — pairing accuracy with low cost. The design integrates five subsystems: a welded frame, a 5 L adhesive tank with toothed spreader, a suction-cup gripper arm, a guide-rail alignment system and an integrated spirit level. Each subsystem was modelled in SolidWorks. Worst-case hand calculations sized the arm (≈40.9 N·m total placement torque for a 3.07 kg, 400 mm tile) and confirmed a 40.2 N suction grip against the 30.1 N tile weight. FEA on the frame, suction-cup holder and adhesive tank (aluminium alloy, silicon and structural steel) verified deformation and equivalent stress stayed within safe limits under operating loads.
